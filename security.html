Web Application Security

Quick answer

Public forms and interactive tools can be targeted by spam, malicious input, and automated abuse. A few basic habits close most of the common gaps.

Common threats

Spam, malicious input, automated abuse, weak passwords, unused accounts, unsafe uploaded files, cross-site request forgery, and suspicious activity can all affect a public website.

How to mitigate them

  • Use HTTPS with an SSL certificate across the entire site.
  • Validate and sanitize all form input on the server, not just in the browser.
  • Use CAPTCHA, honeypot fields, or rate limiting to reduce spam.
  • Use protection against cross-site request forgery for forms.
  • Use strong, unique passwords for hosting, domain, and administrator accounts.
  • Remove unused plug-ins, accounts, themes, and code.
  • Use proper file permissions and security headers.

You have some protections in place, but gaps remain.

Weak passwords and missing multi-factor authentication are the most common way small businesses get locked out of accounts.

Next steps

  1. Turn on multi-factor authentication for email first.
  2. Change any shared or reused passwords this week.
  3. Remove access for anyone who no longer works with your team.

These basics need attention soon.

Right now, one weak password or one missed update could take down access to accounts your business depends on.

Next steps

  1. Change shared passwords and turn on multi-factor authentication today.
  2. Turn on automatic updates for the software your team relies on.
  3. Set a reminder to review account access every few months.
