Backup Readiness Checklist

Before you start

Have a general idea of where your important business files are stored. No account needed. You can save or print your result.

Do you keep a separate copy of important files?

A separate copy means the file is not only stored on the same computer or phone you use every day.

Your backup plan looks solid.

Keep checking the backup on a schedule and make sure someone besides you knows how to recover files.

You have a starting point, but your backup plan has gaps.

Your business has some copies of important files, but recovery steps and backup checks are not clear yet.

Next steps

  1. List the files your business cannot afford to lose.
  2. Choose where the backup copy will live.
  3. Set a regular reminder to check that it works.

Your backup plan needs attention soon.

Important files may not be protected yet. Start with a list of files you cannot afford to lose, then pick one place for the backup copy to live.

Next steps

  1. List the files your business cannot afford to lose.
  2. Choose one backup location and move copies there this week.
  3. Set a recurring reminder to confirm the backup actually ran.

Technology Upgrade Planning Tool

Answer honestly about where your business stands today. This is about planning an upgrade in stages, not replacing everything at once.

Readiness check

You are ready to plan the upgrade in stages.

Start with the smallest piece you can change without disrupting daily work, then expand from there.

You have some pieces in place, but a few gaps remain.

Nail down the parts you are missing before you start moving data or spending money.

Next steps

  1. Write down exactly what problem the upgrade should solve.
  2. Get a rough budget approved before comparing tools.
  3. Decide who will need training and how much time that takes.

It is too early to start the upgrade itself.

Spend time defining the problem and the budget first. An upgrade started without a plan usually costs more and takes longer.

Next steps

  1. Name the specific problem the current tool or system creates.
  2. Talk with the people who will use the new tool daily.
  3. Revisit this checklist once you have a budget in mind.

Shared Files Organization Planner

Check what already exists so you can see what is missing before you reorganize anything.

Current setup

Your shared files are in good shape.

Keep reviewing folder structure and access every few months as your team changes.

Your file system has a structure, but it needs cleanup.

Start with naming and access before adding any new tools.

Next steps

  1. Agree on one folder naming pattern the whole team will use.
  2. Set a recurring time to archive or delete old files.
  3. Review who currently has access and remove what is not needed.

Shared files need a clear structure soon.

Files scattered across devices and messages are hard to back up and easy to lose.

Next steps

  1. Pick one shared location for business files going forward.
  2. Create a short, simple folder structure everyone can follow.
  3. Show every team member where that location is.
