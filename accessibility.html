Accessibility and Inclusive Design

Quick answer

Accessibility is part of the design from the start. People are often blocked by the way a website is designed, not because they are incapable of using technology.

Making websites accessible

All meaningful images include useful alt text. Decorative images are ignored by screen readers. The site uses sufficient text contrast and does not use color as the only way to communicate an error, success message, category, or warning.

The full site works with a keyboard. This includes menus, search, filters, comparison controls, forms, downloads, and feedback features. Keyboard focus stays visible. The site uses a Skip to main content link, real HTML buttons, real links, labeled form fields, and semantic page sections.

Inclusive design

Visitors may use screen readers, magnification, browser zoom, high-contrast settings, a keyboard, switch device, voice control, or another alternative to a mouse. Visitors with cognitive or learning differences may need a predictable layout, clear instructions, fewer distractions, and information broken into smaller pieces.

Class insights

The accessibility target is WCAG 2.2 Level AA. Automated checks can include Lighthouse, WAVE, axe DevTools, and Accessibility Insights. Manual review remains important.
